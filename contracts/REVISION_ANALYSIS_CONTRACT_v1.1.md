# Recovery divide BMC revision contract v1.1

Frozen: 2026-08-11 (America/Chicago)

Purpose: respond to the two independent simulated BMC Medicine reviews without redefining the primary functional construct to rescue a result, excluding a non-replicating cohort, or overwriting the v1.0 submission package.

## Version boundary

- `v1.0` remains an immutable record of the submitted simulation package.
- All revised code, derived data, outputs, reports, and submission assets are written under `revision_round_1/` or version-labelled `revision_v1_1` folders.
- Restricted participant-level data remain outside any release or submission ZIP.
- Every revised manuscript number must be generated from a saved aggregate output and listed in the evidence ledger.

## Scientific reframing

1. The eight cohort-by-exposure education and wealth comparisons are the primary analysis and must be visible in the main paper.
2. Wealth within low education is a focused secondary analysis. It will not be presented as preregistered and will not define the title.
3. The principal outcome label is `functional-difficulty-free life expectancy` (FDFLE), because the primary state is any reported difficulty in a common ADL/IADL set.
4. Causal or capacity language is prohibited. Use `observed recovery`, `recovery-related contribution`, `wealth-associated post-onset difference`, and `less durable observed independence`.
5. Internal promotion and stopping rules remain in a protocol-amendment appendix only; inference is presented through continuous effect estimates, uncertainty intervals, and between-cohort heterogeneity.

## Estimands

### Primary population-initialised estimand

At exact age 60, initialise each cohort and socioeconomic group using its model-standardised probability of functional difficulty at age 60. Independent occupancy enters `I0`; difficulty occupancy enters `D1`. Report:

- total life expectancy to age 100;
- FDFLE (`I0 + R1` years);
- years with functional difficulty (`D1 + D2` years);
- FDFLE as a percentage of remaining life;
- low-minus-high differences with uncertainty intervals.

Decompose the low-minus-high population FDFLE difference into six Shapley blocks: initial-state composition, onset, recovery, relapse, post-difficulty mortality, and pre-difficulty mortality.

### Secondary transition-conditional estimand

Retain the all-`I0` life table only as `FDFLE conditional on being observed functionally independent at age 60`. It estimates differences generated by subsequent transition rates under a shared starting state and is not called population FDFLE.

## Exposure timing correction

- Correct the audited CHARLS wealth mapping: waves 1 and 2 use `hh1atotb` and `hh2atotb`; waves 3 and 4 use `h3atotb` and `h4atotb`.
- Fix wealth at the first valid value at or after age 60, as before, but exclude every wealth-analysis interval whose origin precedes that measurement.
- Report time from analytic entry to first valid wealth, wealth missingness, and whether wealth was first measured after the first observed difficulty.
- Use entry-wave household identifiers for household-cluster resampling.

## Frozen sensitivity modules

The following are design checks, not result-triggered rescue analyses:

1. incident-inception cohort: first eligible functional observation independent, so recovery follows an observed incident difficulty;
2. low-left-truncation cohort: entry age 60--64;
3. functional thresholds in the focused low-education analysis: ADL-only and at least two difficulties, each with full decomposition and bootstrap intervals;
4. confirmed-state analysis: a destination functional state must be observed at two consecutive interviews; prioritise ELSA and HRS if four-cohort support is inadequate;
5. pre-COVID intervals ending before 2020;
6. common interval support of 1.5--3.25 years;
7. common calendar support (2002--2018 where cohort data permit);
8. continuous-time panel multistate transition-intensity sensitivity, with no claim that it reproduces the discrete-time Shapley decomposition;
9. age-95 versus age-100 truncation/extrapolation check;
10. person- versus household-cluster bootstrap comparison; use available PSU/stratum information as an additional ELSA/HRS design sensitivity, not as an unsupported four-cohort claim.

## Uncertainty and heterogeneity

- Binding revised intervals use household-cluster bootstrap where a household identifier is available; the resampling unit is never an interval.
- Recalculate wealth cut-points inside each replicate.
- Use at least 500 valid cluster replicates and quantify Monte Carlo stability by comparing 250- and 500-replicate endpoints. Increase to 1,000 only if the endpoint drift exceeds 0.10 years or 20% of the reported interval half-width.
- Between-cohort heterogeneity is assessed without choosing MHAS post hoc as the sole reference: report an omnibus statistic and all six pairwise contrasts, using independent-cohort uncertainty.

## Decision rule for the revised paper

- `GO BMC Medicine`: the primary population-initialised analysis shows a supported recovery-related contribution in at least two cohorts, and the focused secondary result remains directionally coherent under exposure-timing correction plus at least one misclassification-resistant analysis.
- `PIVOT`: population FDFLE gradients remain but recovery-related contributions are confined to the broad any-difficulty definition or fail the incident-inception/confirmed-state checks. Reframe around transition decomposition and target a specialist epidemiology/gerontology journal.
- `STOP recovery-divide headline`: the corrected exposure timing reverses or removes the recovery contribution in the previously positive cohorts, or the contribution is explained by initial-state composition alone.

No threshold, state definition, cohort, or time window may be changed after these rules are frozen except to correct a documented coding error, which must be logged.

## Non-substantive estimand-label clarification (2026-08-11)

The primary integration from exact age 60 through age 100 is labelled `truncated remaining years through age 100`, including truncated total life years, FDFLE, and years with functional difficulty. It is not described as complete lifetime expectancy. Two frozen tail checks extend integration through age 110 while holding annual hazards at age 95 or fitting through age 100 and then holding hazards at age 100. This wording clarification does not alter the state definition, contrast, model, or primary numerical integration.
